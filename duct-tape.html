Want a Startup Idea? Look for the Duct Tape

One of the best ways to find a startup idea is to look for where the duct tape is.

But what does “duct tape” even mean?

In the startup world, duct tape refers to the scrappy workarounds people build because no good solution exists (or at least no one knows about it). Duct tape usually isn’t scalable, secure, or elegant. But it solves the problem right now. And when more and more people start using the same duct tape, it signals two things:

  1. There’s real demand to solve this problem.
  2. There’s urgency, because the problem sucks enough that people won’t wait.

Some products you know started as duct tape

  1. Theragun. A chiropractor wanted to treat his own severe pain after a motorcycle accident. He couldn’t find a device for muscle relief, so he attached a modified cushioned head to a Kawasaki jigsaw. That percussive jigsaw became the G1, Theragun’s first model.
  2. Facebook Marketplace. Moms in Facebook Groups were buying and selling kids’ stuff to each other because buying new was expensive. That was the duct tape. Deb Liu, the only PM at Facebook who was a mom at the time, was in those groups and saw it firsthand. She also noticed buying and selling in groups taking off in Asia. When she dug in, far more people were using Facebook Groups to buy, sell, and trade than anyone expected.
  3. GoPro. A surfer was frustrated that he couldn’t capture high-quality action shots of himself surfing. So he used his mom’s old sewing machine to stitch a camera strap out of an old wetsuit, tethered an off-the-shelf camera to his wrist, and paddled out. That’s how GoPro was born.

So how do you actually find the duct tape?

  1. Look at Reddit threads. Reddit is full of free ideas if you know where to look. Go into professional subreddits and sort by top posts to see what people are complaining about or hacking around. Interested in a wealth management startup? Check r/personalfinance or r/FIRE. See what people hate about their current apps, or what manual spreadsheets they’re building instead. r/Spreadsheets is another good window into problems being solved by hand right now.
  2. Look for templates. A popular template is proof of demand for a workaround. Templates exist because no software does the job natively, or people won’t pay for the software that does. Filter for bestsellers on Gumroad and Etsy — download counts tell you exactly how big the demand is. Commonly shared templates on LinkedIn work the same way.
  3. Look at what people pay humans to do manually. Browse Fiverr gigs, Upwork postings, and “hire a VA to do X” threads. When people pay a person to do a repetitive task, that’s duct tape with a price tag attached. You already know the willingness to pay.
  4. Read the 3-star reviews. App Store and G2 reviews of incumbent products are complaint mines. The 3-star ones are the gold: “I use it anyway because nothing better exists” is the exact duct-tape signal you’re looking for.
  5. Look for Zapier chains and Chrome extensions. When people wire four tools together with Zapier or install an extension to patch a product’s gap, that’s duct tape in software form. Bonus: the usage numbers are often public.

One caveat: not every piece of duct tape is a startup. The workaround has to be frequent and painful enough that people would pay to make it disappear.

But the duct tape is everywhere once you start looking. The founders who win are usually just the ones who noticed it first.
